# Guided Review interface

## Overview

This records the incumbent **Operate** surface inside BB. [app.tsx](app.tsx) registers the sidebar panel, with [TitleBarControls](components/TitleBarControls.tsx) in its title bar, and an app overlay that adds Needs review and pinned reviews to BB's thread list; [Inbox](components/inbox/Inbox.tsx) (All reviews) and [ReviewWorkspace](components/ReviewWorkspace.tsx) provide the panel's two working views. The design inherits BB’s typography, semantic colors, controls, and code themes. It establishes no separate marketing identity.

The review owns the screen. On a desktop or an iPad, BB's title bar and one 44px band sit above the code; on a phone, BB's bar above and one dock below. Everything else opens on demand and closes itself. Each job has one home: the review's commands in BB's title bar, where you are at the left, conversation and finishing at the right, how the code draws in Diff options, and facts about the PR on the Overview.

## Colors

The frontmatter retains live host token references so light and dark themes remain authoritative; no palette colors or `dark:` variants are used. Neutral surfaces, text tiers, borders, and hover/active fills carry the layout. Color marks meaning, in small doses, through one tone per status ([Badge](components/ui/badge.tsx)): primary for the main action, focus, the open chapter, "ready", and your own words; success for approved, passing, viewed, and resolved; warning for in progress, pending, medium risk, changes requested, and a comment on an older diff; danger for failures, closed PRs, and high risk; merged for merged PRs; agent (the terminal's bright magenta) for anything the assistant wrote or does. Fills are 10–15% tints; text uses the theme's contrast-safe text roles (`diff-added`, `warning-text`, `destructive-text`). Status text and icons accompany color.

[DiffViewer](components/DiffViewer.tsx) uses BB’s light/dark code-theme names when supplied and receives the current mode from the SDK. Preserve that connection rather than hardcoding a syntax palette.

## Typography

Interface text inherits the BB sans family; file paths, repositories, branches, line references, and CLI examples use monospace, with a path's directory dimmed so its file name leads. Agent-written text (the guide's intent, comments, discussions, and assistant answers) renders through BB's Markdown, as in chat; titles and chapter summaries render their `code` spans inline. All reviews has no title of its own: BB's title bar names the page. The Overview's title uses the base size, one step larger where the Overview is at least 640px wide. Review titles and controls predominantly use the small scale, with extra-small metadata and compact 10–11px badges; Finish review's part headings are 11px semibold capitals. Headings rely on medium/semibold weight and modest size changes. Keep explanations readable and reserve truncation for titles and paths with constrained space. A line of facts ([MetaLine](components/MetaLine.tsx)) separates them with "·" and breaks only after one.

## Layout

All reviews is a centered column up to 1024px wide, with 6px padding, 24px where the page is at least 640px wide; it scrolls inside the panel, which BB clips to the window. One find-or-paste box leads it on a wide page; where the page is short of room, as on a phone, ⌕ in BB's title bar opens the box at the top, with Cancel. Then the sections, each a sticky heading with its count and a chevron that collapses it (remembered in this browser): Needs review, open, in three parts under small headings (Back to you, New, Team requests); then Waiting on author, Reviewed, and Archive, closed. A query opens them all. Rows are two lines: the title (with a pin when pinned), the repository and number where the list is at least 720px wide, and the age; then the status line, which leads with why it's yours and adds only what changes the decision to open it, followed by the repository where the list is narrower. A stack's rows sit together, bottom first, behind a left rule. With no reviews, a first-run panel takes the sections' place.

The review workspace fills the panel's height and width, and lays itself out from its own size, never the window's, the user agent, or the orientation ([workspace-layout](lib/workspace-layout.ts), with 16px of hysteresis), in three modes:

- **Tablet** (a desktop and an iPad, and the unfolded iPhone Duo): the review's commands in BB's title bar, a 44px band, and the navigator (the chapters) at the left and the side panel (Comments, Finish review, or the review agent) at the right, each docked or over the code.
- **Phone** (under 600px wide): nothing in BB's title bar, no band, a dock at the bottom, and one sheet. The unfolded iPhone Duo held sideways beside BB's thread list (631px) is a tablet.
- **Short** (under 500px tall, such as a phone held sideways): a 40px band, the sides only over the code, the chapter intro collapsed.

Docking keeps the code at least 560px wide. The navigator is 288px by default (200–560) and the panel 400px (320–640); both widths are global. The panel gets the room first: it docks, narrower down to 320px where that keeps the code at 560px (391px on an unfolded iPhone Duo held sideways, 951px wide); otherwise it opens over the code from the right, `min(440px, 92%)` wide. The navigator docks while the code keeps 560px beside it and the docked panel; with no room beside the docked panel it waits, and docks again when the panel closes; with no panel it opens over the code from the left, `min(320px, 85%)` wide. Docking is instant; a side over the code slides in over 200ms above a 40% black scrim, with no motion under reduced motion, and Esc, a tap on the scrim, ✕, or a pick closes it. Sides never open over the code by themselves: only docked ones open again where they were. Resizing BB's pane, or an iPad's or a desktop's window, hides a docked side that no longer fits and docks it again with room. A phone's window resizing (a fold, an unfold, or a rotation, on a coarse pointer, with the window's shorter side under 744px before and after) carries a docked panel over the code or into the sheet instead, so what's open stays open; while you type elsewhere, it hides rather than cover the box. The line between a docked side and the code drags, or steps with the arrow keys; double-click or double-tap restores the default width.

The band runs across the top of all three columns: the navigator's head (the chapters toggle beside Find in review's box), the reader's bar, and the panel's header (its title, its actions, and ✕). The reader's bar holds the chapters toggle and Find while the navigator isn't docked, then the breadcrumb (‹ All reviews / the review's title / the chapter's step and title, or Overview), then the compare select, Fold comments, and Diff options at its end; in full screen, the review's commands after a rule. What shows is measured against the room: the chapter's title first, then the compare select (full or brief label), then "All reviews" beside ‹ from 720px, then the review's title; Fold comments gives way first, since Diff options holds it too. On a phone's touch screen (its window's shorter side under 744, the unfolded Duo included), a band with under 600px of room ends with Diff options alone, which then holds the compare select and Fold comments, off the fold; an iPad's band fits as a mouse's does. One notice strip sits under the band: the most pressing notice, tinted by its tone, with +N for the rest.

The content scrolls in one box between the sides: the Overview, capped at 768px, or a chapter (its intro, its files, and its end). Diffs are unified whenever their pane is narrower than 960px, so neither side of a split clips its code, and long lines wrap by default where the code is narrower than 720px. Long code scrolls within its file. The selection bar and the step bar float at the bottom of the workspace, at the code's right; on touch, beside a docked panel with under 600px of code, they sit at its left, up to 420px wide.

A phone's dock is 52px plus the bottom safe area: ‹ All reviews, the chapter you're on (its ring, its title, and a chevron, filling the rest), Comments (the count not submitted, and a dot when something needs you), Ask agent, and Full screen. Its tabs switch one sheet, which rises from the dock to 12px under the workspace's top, with a drag handle (a flick, or a drag of a quarter of its height, closes it) and a header (the chapters sheet's is its Chapters | All files switch); the code behind it is inert. The dock hides while you type or select lines, and BB's toasts are lifted above it. In phone and short modes, popovers open as drawers from the bottom.

Full screen uses the browser's full screen where it can; where it can't, as on an iPhone, the workspace covers the window from the top layer, with its overlays inside it. BB's title bar is hidden then, so the band ends with the review's commands.

The workspace pads by each safe area on its own side (`--gr-safe-*`, from `env(safe-area-inset-*)`), so a home-screen app keeps clear of the notch, the Duo's side rail, and the home indicator; popovers keep that far from each edge too. An on-screen keyboard is followed through `visualViewport` (`--gr-kb`): the workspace ends above it and brings the box you type in into view; the box comes with its buttons (`data-keyboard-box`) where they fit, else its text box's top, and then whole once the keyboard goes away, unless you've scrolled it out of view. Where BB shrinks its pane to the visual viewport instead, the keyboard counts as up all the same, and a pane that only got shorter while you type keeps its height for the mode, until it grows back as the keyboard goes (up to a second). In a phone's window, when the keyboard leaves no more room than a phone held sideways has, the band, the notices, the file's sticky row, and Comments' Finish bar give way to the box. An iPad keeps its band. On a foldable ([use-fold](lib/use-fold.ts)), the band's controls, the selection and step bars, menus, and a panel over the code keep off the fold: where the browser reports it (Viewport Segments), that; without the API, the window's middle ±24px on the unfolded iPhone Duo held sideways, and for menus held upright in a home-screen app, and 40–52% of the window's height for menus held upright in Safari. These estimates are to check on a real Duo.

## Elevation & Depth

Thin host borders separate the band, the notices, the sides, and the files. Docked sides sit flush: the navigator on the raised surface, the panel on the background. A side over the code and the phone's sheet are opaque (the host background at full alpha, so a translucent host theme can't show through), with the strongest shadow, over a 40% black scrim. Cards (diff files, comment cards, the chapter intro, settings sections) use the host card surface with a hairline shadow; a comment in the code adds a soft shadow over the code. A file's slim row sticks to the top of the scrolling code. The selection bar, the step bar, and the "need you" pill float on the popover surface with a strong shadow. Button fills respond immediately on hover and transition out over 150ms through the shared motion helper. Sides and sheets slide in over 200ms; nothing slides with reduced motion. The floating review-agent window uses stronger elevation.

## Shapes

Controls use the host medium radius; cards, comment cards, and icon tiles use the large radius; the phone sheet's top corners are rounded. Pills identify state, risk, CI, and file kind; the step bar and the "need you" pill are fully rounded. A comment's frame says where it lives: dashed with a 3px solid edge while it's only in BB, solid on GitHub, muted when private (see Components). Icons remain compact and secondary to the task labels; the chapters toggle is a list glyph, never BB's sidebar glyph, and Diff options is a slider glyph, not a gear.

## Do's and Don'ts

- Do reuse host tokens, shared controls, visible focus, and explicit loading/error states.
- Do keep the code first: BB's bar and one band (a phone: BB's bar and the dock) are the only standing chrome; everything else opens on demand and closes itself.
- Do lay out from the workspace's own size, and dock a side only where the code keeps 560px; otherwise open it over the code, and never by itself.
- Do give every control a 44px target on any coarse pointer, iPads included, with [touch.ts](components/touch.ts): `TOUCH_CONTROL` for a control, `TOUCH_COMPACT` for a 36px button in a 48px or 40px bar, `TOUCH_TARGET` for a text link or a small ✕ that keeps its size.
- Do say where a comment lives in its frame and its words: dashed and "not submitted" in BB, solid with its author on GitHub, muted with a lock when private.
- Do keep toggles where they are: the chapters toggle beside Find in review, the review's commands in BB's title bar, Full screen last.
- Do keep color semantic: one tone per meaning, as tints and small marks, never as large fills.
- Do make server account scope and GitHub write actions clear where users act: what posts now (Send reply, Resolve) says so, and Submit says where it posts and as whom.
- Do keep what's open, typed, and focused through a fold, an unfold, a rotation, and a resize, and through BB mounting the page anew as its layout switches at 768px ([remount](lib/remount.ts): kept in sessionStorage, not until 300ms after a window resize, so BB's old layout at the window's new size doesn't count; carried within 2s, never across a reload).
- Don't put a second bar over the code, squeeze the code beside a docked side, or let anything open over the code by itself.
- Don't put tappable controls on a foldable's fold, or under a home-screen app's notch, rail, or home indicator.
- Don't use the kit's `max-md` touch guard for the plugin's controls: an iPad is a touch screen too.
- Don’t imply separate team accounts, remote-machine checkout support, or shared review storage beyond the actual BB installation.

## Review lifecycle

Generation and the submitted verdict are stored separately. Successful submissions survive refresh and regeneration. A supervised service reads GitHub in batches (every two minutes for reviews in progress, sooner when GitHub notifies about a PR); Refresh and returning focus to the list also reconcile state. Only the authenticated viewer’s review supplies their verdict: their latest approve or changes-requested review, never a reply. Whose turn it is comes from one pure rule set (lib/turn.ts) shared by the server and the panel, and All reviews, the Overview, the navigator, and BB's thread list say it in the same words (lib/status-line.ts). A push alone never makes it the reviewer's turn; a re-request, a question or mention, feedback handled after new commits, or (opt-in) the assistant's all-addressed check does. Snooze until the author responds waits for a newer reason; a timed snooze waits until its time, or a newer reason. Each reason alerts once through Needs You, after the re-review is prepared, and opening, snoozing, or acting on the review clears it. Terminal PRs appear in Archive, and Comments' foot opens their archived review. ## Review customization

Settings are accessible from All reviews (⚙ in BB's title bar), a review's ⋯ (Review settings…), and BB plugin settings; the page's bar goes back where it was opened from. Separate setup (Ready to review, with the GitHub account), guide notifications, guide generation, assistant behavior, whose turn, re-reviews (what a re-review opens on, Changes since your review or All changes, as the Settings page's other choices), BB's thread list, and trust into simple sections; the thread-list section's one toggle says why to turn it off: BB has no slot there, so an update can break it. Trust chooses whose PRs agents run on unattended and holds the opt-in to let the review agent run the repo's tests and linters, saying it runs the PR's code. Settings are explicit-save, and restoring defaults is staged. How the code draws (layout, wrapping, whitespace, GitHub threads, folded comments) is set in a review's Diff options, for every review, not in Settings. Private notes autosave with recoverable text and non-destructive conflict comparison.

Each review keeps, in this browser, its chapter or Overview, its scroll position, its side panel's view, and the turn it last opened in, so it opens on its Overview only when it comes back to you; every review shares the navigator's and the panel's widths, whether the chapters are wanted, Chapters or All files, and the Diff options. State saved by 0.5 moves over once: the sidebar's width becomes the navigator's, its open tool becomes the side panel, and a review left on Feedback or Threads opens Comments. Derive an opaque page background from the host background color so a translucent host theme cannot show through the review, its sides, or the sheet.
